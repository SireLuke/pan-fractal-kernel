## Executive result

All three requested mathematical experiments produced positive, bounded results:
1. Du Val's constrained ten-coordinate realization, interpreted using the congruence class confirmed by Coxeter's displayed example vectors, reconstructs the E8 lattice exactly after orthonormal coordinate reduction and scaling by 1/5.
2. The E8 nearest-root graph was breadth-first traversed through graph depth 4: 996,001 unique lattice vertices in total. A deterministic replay reproduced every shell count and the same SHA-256 digest for shell 4.

## Experiment 1 — Du Val constrained R10 representation equals E8

### Source and coordinate model

Coxeter, “Regular and Semi-Regular Polytopes III,” Math. Z. 200 (1988), §3.8, pp. 38–39, describes Du Val's ten-coordinate realization of the 5_21 lattice. The coordinates lie in Euclidean R10 and satisfy:
- x1+x2+x3+x4+x5 = 0
- x6+x7+x8+x9+x10 = 0

Coxeter gives a congruence condition modulo 5. The searchable PDF text has typographic OCR ambiguities in the equality chain, so the implementation was cross-checked against the explicit displayed coordinate examples in the same table. Those examples satisfy the common-residue form:
x1 ≡ x2 ≡ x3 ≡ x4 ≡ x5 ≡ 2x6 ≡ 2x7 ≡ 2x8 ≡ 2x9 ≡ 2x10 (mod 5).

This is the condition used for the independent enumeration in this experiment. Before a production adapter is implemented, compare the congruence glyphs against a high-resolution scan of the original page.

### Exact reduction and lattice covolume

Parameterize the constrained space by y=(x1,x2,x3,x4,x6,x7,x8,x9), with x5 and x10 fixed by the two zero-sum constraints. The congruence condition gives:
y ≡ c(1,1,1,1,3,3,3,3) (mod 5), for c in Z/5Z.

Thus the integer coordinate lattice in y-space is generated by g=(1,1,1,1,3,3,3,3) and 5e2,...,5e8, with determinant 5^7.

The induced Euclidean Gram matrix on y is block diagonal with two blocks I4+J4. Each block has determinant 5, so det(G)=25 and sqrt(det(G))=5. The ten-dimensional constrained lattice therefore has covolume 5^7 × 5 = 5^8 in its eight-dimensional Euclidean subspace. After scaling coordinates by 1/5, its covolume is exactly 1.

For any such lattice vector, the squared norm divided by 25 is an even integer (follows from the zero-sum constraints and the common-residue condition). Exhaustive enumeration of all integer points with squared norm <=50 found:
- one zero vector at norm squared 0;
- no nonzero vectors of norm squared below 50;
- exactly 240 vectors at norm squared 50.

After scaling by 1/5, the minimum squared norm is 2 and there are exactly 240 minimal vectors.

### Independent 240-vector root-system check

An orthonormal basis for the two zero-sum blocks was constructed by QR orthogonalization. The 240 scaled Du Val vectors were transformed into R8.

Results:
- 240 unique vectors;
- every vector has squared norm 2;
- inner-product distribution relative to a fixed vector: -2:1, -1:56, 0:126, +1:56, +2:1;
- all 240 root reflections preserve the full 240-vector set;
- rank 8 and covolume 1.

This identifies the scaled constrained lattice as the even unimodular rank-8 E8 lattice. It is not a 10-dimensional lattice: R10 is a redundant ambient coordinate representation with two independent linear constraints.

**Status: PASS**, subject to the noted high-resolution source-glyph verification before productionizing the congruence parser.

## Experiment 2 — E8 nearest-neighbor BFS depth

The graph vertices are E8 lattice points. Edges connect p to p+alpha for each of the 240 E8 roots alpha. Coordinates were stored as exact integers scaled by 2, avoiding floating-point accumulation.

BFS shell results (graph word distance from origin):

| Graph shell | New vertices | Cumulative vertices |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 240 | 241 |
| 2 | 9,120 | 9,361 |
| 3 | 121,680 | 131,041 |
| 4 | 864,960 | 996,001 |

Shell 4 contains points with Euclidean squared norms from 14 through 32 (in the unscaled canonical E8 coordinates); graph distance is not the same as Euclidean radius.

Deterministic replay repeated the BFS with sorted frontier traversal:
- shell counts reproduced exactly: [240, 9120, 121680, 864960];
- cumulative unique vertices: 996,001;
- shell-4 SHA-256: bf8c1e9c401effe87d03ea3fe74050439d022c03a5872ab638a4524150af697a;
- replay digest matched exactly.

The infinite graph has no finite maximum graph depth: the E8 roots include a basis of the rank-8 E8 lattice, so every lattice point is reachable by a finite word in root steps, and the lattice contains infinitely many points. Depth 4 is the largest shell completed in this run; depth 5 and beyond were not executed.

**Status: PASS** for exact finite-radius enumeration and deterministic replay through depth 4. No depth-5 count or memory benchmark is claimed.

## Experiment 3 — Coxeter plane and 72 Sierpinski seed orbits

### Projection construction

Built a Coxeter element from eight simple-root reflections. Its eigenvalue pair exp(±2πi/30) defines the Coxeter plane. The induced action on that plane is a rotation by 12 degrees.

The 240 E8 roots project into eight concentric rings, each containing 30 points.

Enumerated all 60,480 triangular faces of Gosset 4_21 using the root adjacency graph. Results in the Coxeter plane:
- 2,160 projected faces are equilateral under the numerical tolerance 1e-7;
- these form exactly 72 Coxeter orbits;
- every orbit has length 30;
- the 72 × 30 orbit members account for all 2,160 projected equilateral faces.

Control experiment: five independently sampled random orthonormal 2-planes were used with the same triangle set and tolerance. Each produced zero equilateral projected faces. This supports the special role of the Coxeter plane, but five controls are not a theorem about every other plane.

### Sierpinski recursion

For a projected equilateral seed with vertices v0,v1,v2, use the three contractions:
F_i(x)=(x+v_i)/2, i=0,1,2.

The complete 30-member Coxeter orbit is equivariant under the 12-degree Coxeter rotation: rotating a seed and applying its corresponding labeled contractions gives the rotated child pattern. Taking all 72 orbits preserves the Coxeter symmetry; using one isolated seed does not preserve the full 30-fold orbit symmetry.

For the 72 chosen orbit representatives, the first subdivision generated 648 child-vertex occurrences. After deduplication:
- 307 unique projected points;
- 91 points coincide with projected E8 root vertices;
- 216 distinct midpoint locations are not in the projected 240-root set.

The latter are genuine new points in the projection plane, not E8 lattice vertices. The descendants belong to the Sierpinski IFS geometry, not to the original finite Gosset vertex set. Each seed's level-n subdivision has 3^n subtriangles; the retained area fraction is (3/4)^n and the limiting Sierpinski dimension is log(3)/log(2).

**Status: PASS** for Coxeter-plane face detection, orbit decomposition, rotation equivariance, and first-level non-lattice descendant check. The 72 orbits are a projection-selected seed family, not a new subpolytope or a claim that the Gosset polytope itself is a Sierpinski fractal.
